This file records the legal-requirements investigation behind the audit changes.
It is intentionally lightweight: the substantive requirements are reflected in the
updated documents themselves and in the implementation checks below. Review notes
are kept here so a later qualified legal review has a clear starting point.

## 1. Markets and target user

Product signals used to scope the audit:

- The app is distributed as an Android APK and has a close-testing / waitlist flow,
  not a public consumer store listing in the codebase. The public distribution story
  is still a deployment/content problem, not something this audit can settle.
- The stated target customer market is adults aged 18-30.
- Internationalization is implemented for many locales, but that is a content/i18n
  posture, not evidence of a launched consumer offering in every those markets.

Audit conclusion:

- Primary scope: United Kingdom.
- Secondary scope: European Union / EEA, because the product posture and i18n treat
  EU languages as first-class and the business has not stated otherwise.
- Other countries are not separately evidenced as supported markets by the codebase,
  so they are treated as out of scope unless a launch decision is made.

This audit therefore prioritises:

- UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018.
- UK consumer law, principally the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Consumer
  Contracts Regulations 2013 for digital content and distance contracts.
- Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 consumer-rights and disclosure
  directions where relevant to the next consumer-facing launch.
- EU law: GDPR, Consumer Rights Directive as implemented, UK-style parity considerations,
  and the Digital Services Act for any consumer-facing online interface that would be in scope.
- Age-appropriate design expectations generally, even though the target market is adults.
  The product must still avoid nudging minors into purchases and must not present itself
  as suitable for children.

This is a requirements scan, not legal advice. Several questions are explicitly flagged
as unresolved and requiring qualified review before launch.

## 2. Privacy and personal data

Governing law:

- If operating in the UK: UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018.
- If operating in the EU/EEA: EU GDPR.
- Cross-border: the business must be able to say where controller/processor relationships
  actually sit and which safeguards apply.

Key requirements the audit looked for:

- A lawful basis for processing, with the basis actually matching the processing.
- Transparency: privacy information that is accurate, specific and available before/
  at the point data is collected.
- Valid consent where consent is the basis, granular, informed, and revocable.
- Special-category data handling only where justified and disclosed.
- Account deletion and data-subject rights with a workable process.
- Retention and security posture that matches the product.
- Breach-response readiness and a contact point.

What the current product collects, based on this repo's behaviour rather than assumed:

- Account identity: email and password/sign-in transcript, plus whatever account profile
  fields exist (name, avatar, sport, goals, etc.).
- App usage and feature usage: plans generated, workouts logged, AI requests, analysis
  results, credit/balance state, purchases/top-ups.
- Performance data entered or generated by the app: athlete goals, availability, plan
  adjustments, workout completions, metrics/insights outputs.
- Uploaded form videos where that feature is used, plus analysis results and metadata.
- Health/wearable syncs only where the user actively connects a provider.
- Safety reports and support requests submitted by users.
- Analytics/monitoring where the app or backend sends it.

What is probably NOT special-category data by default:

- Generic athletic performance records (times, completed workouts, plan adherence) are
  usually not special-category health data on their own.
- Health-related data becomes more sensitive when it includes explicit health status,
  injury information, medical disclosures, diagnostic-style outputs, or health/wearable
  sync that reveals health conditions.

The right framing:

- Safety reports, injury-related inputs, and wearable health data can implicate special
  category data depending on content. The privacy policy should not assume that every
  athletic record is special-category data and should not claim the opposite where the
  product can actually receive sensitive health content.
- AI coaching outputs and video analysis results are informational; they should not be
  represented as medical or clinical data unless the product truly makes that claim.

## 3. Consumer rights and digital services

Relevant consumer concepts:

- Digital services with recurring charges must be described clearly, including renewal,
  cancellation, price and what the user actually gets.
- Distance selling and pre-contract information matter for app purchases and top-ups.
- Statutory rights are not removable by contract terms.
- Unfair or misleading terms/representations are a risk, especially around:
  - refund exclusions,
  - "no refunds ever" language,
  - blanket liability waivers,
  - promises that AI features are always accurate, safe, or clinically useful.

Concrete project-specific reading:

- The product has subscription-style plans and credit/coin top-ups and a Market, so
  purchase terms and refunds need to be specific to the actual charge model.
- The audit must reflect charge-after-success purchase behaviour rather than invent
  payment timing that does not exist.

## 4. Online purchases, credits and refunds

What the documentation must match:

- Credit system mechanics.
- How credits are earned, loaded, spent, and rolled over or limited.
- Purchase terms for credits/top-ups and for subscriptions.
- Whether charges happen before or after the service succeeds.
- Failed-service handling: what happens if a feature request is initiated and the
  service does not complete.
- Refund policy: what is refundable, when, how to request it, and what statutory rights
  remain. No invented expiry dates or invented payment methods.
- Support process for purchase issues, so the policy is not describing a process that
  does not exist in the app.

The audit is explicit that terms must not imply remedies or processes the product does
not implement.

## 5. AI-generated advice and transparency

Relevant themes:

- Transparency that AI is used.
- Appropriate disclaimer that AI outputs are informational.
- No promise that AI recommendations are always accurate, safe, or correctly personalised.
- No substitution for qualified coaching or medical advice where that is the real question.
- Where the product does AI coaching, training advice, or form/Video analysis, the
  relevant disclosure must exist and be findable.

Project-specific AI features to disclose appropriately:

- AI training/plan generation.
- AI-related analysis outputs such as video form analysis and other coaching/analysis flows.
- Nutrition, injury and mental-wellbeing content where AI is involved.

## 6. Marketing, cookies and tracking

Relevant themes:

- Cookie/transparency notices where the product sets non-essential cookies or uses
  non-essential tracking.
- Consent where required, recorded and respected.
- Marketing communications described accurately and subject to user choice.

The audit checked whether there is a live cookie consent system in the app. A CookieNotice
component and cookie-related legal URLs exist, but the audit must verify whether there is a
real consent/record mechanism or only a notice. If the latter, the policy should not claim
more than the product does.

## 7. Account deletion and data retention

Relevant themes:

- A deletion flow that actually works.
- A public-facing deletion URL if the store requires one.
- Retention practices that are stated honestly.
- Distinction between immediate deletion, pending deletion, and kept data that must be
  retained for legal/compliance reasons.

## 8. Security incidents and data breaches

Relevant themes:

- A breach-response process.
- A contact/disclosure path for security issues.
- Honest representation of the posture without claiming certifications that do not exist.

## 9. App-store requirements

Relevant themes:

- Privacy policy URL.
- Any required content ratings, data-use disclosures, and store-listed policy links.
- Age-targeting and content-appropriate behaviour.
- No built-in assumption that publishing to a store is complete just because documents exist.

## 10. Unresolved legal questions to flag for qualified review

These are not settled by this audit. They are recorded for a qualified solicitor/data
protection adviser to review before launch:

- Whether the current top-up/subscription/refund wording fully satisfies the Consumer
  Contracts Regulations 2013 and DMCC 2024 pre-contract and renewal disclosure expectations
  for the intended launch markets.
- Whether a non-trivial video upload + AI analysis flow raises special-category or
  biometric-adjacent considerations in any target market, and what the lawful basis and
  safeguards should be.
- Whether the cookie/transparency posture meets consent requirements for non-essential
  tracking in the UK/EU, or should be reduced to what the app actually does.
- Whether the age-targeting and marketing approach is appropriate for the intended 18-30
  market, including any marketing-to-minors and age-gating questions.
- The exact controller identity, processing locations, sub-processor list, and transfer
  safeguards to publish in the privacy policy once the real deployment is settled.
- Whether any app-store listings, if launched, need additional data-safety disclosures
  beyond the documents in this repo.
- Whether any competition/promotion mechanics (if used) need separate terms beyond the
  current competition-terms page.

## 11. Scope boundaries for this prompt

What this audit does:

- Scope the requirements.
- Update the core legal documents to match actual product behaviour.
- Improve findability, versioning, and disclosure placement.
- Flag unresolved items.

What this audit deliberately does not do:

- Claim verified legal compliance.
- Invent company details, addresses, certifications, or provider terms.
- Change retention behaviour or delete existing user data without a verified plan.
- Assume store launch is complete.
