Why the generated text can be wrong

  • It is confidently wrong, not obviously wrong. A language model rarely says "I don't know". It will produce fluent, specific, plausible guidance that is simply not right — or not suitable — for you as an individual athlete.
  • Expect errors, omissions and incorrect assumptions. A generated answer can be simply wrong, can leave out something that matters, and can assume things about you that are not true. It can rest on information that is out of date. Treat all of that as ordinary, not as a fault you have triggered.
  • Outdated information is a real failure mode. A model's own knowledge has a cut-off, so it can repeat guidance that has since changed, or general practice that was never right for your sport or your event.
  • What you enter decides what comes out. If what you have given the app is incomplete, inaccurate or out of date, the recommendation built on it is likely to be unsuitable as well. Nothing here independently knows your health, your medical history or your athletic condition — it knows only what your account contains.
  • It cannot see you. It knows what you logged. It does not know how you move, how you slept, what you ate, or what happened today.
  • It does not know your medical history, your medication, an injury you never mentioned, a condition you have not been diagnosed with, or a pregnancy.
  • It is not a clinician and it is not you. It has no legal or professional duty of care towards you. It has no obligation to be accurate, and no way of knowing what it got wrong.

Stop and see a professional if

  • You have sharp, worsening or persistent pain
  • You have an injury, or are returning from one
  • You are pregnant, or have a medical condition that affects exercise
  • You are under 18 and considering a restrictive diet — nutrition plans are already restricted to 18+ for this reason
  • You have a history of disordered eating, or the app's output makes you feel worse rather than better
  • You are taking medication that affects what you can safely do
  • Anything in the app contradicts what a doctor or physiotherapist has told you

What the app does about this

  • Nutrition plans are blocked for under-18s in code, with a refusal message rather than a silent block.
  • Injury Management never diagnoses. The tab's own scaffold is fixed material compiled by us and calculated on your device; the personalised plan is written by the model from what you report, and it is instructed never to name a tissue, a grade or a condition and never to promise a recovery date. Its red flags come first, it says a qualified professional must supervise it, and the screen carries a permanent notice telling you to see a physiotherapist or doctor for sharp, persistent or worsening pain.
  • You are told what you are seeing. The Safety Information page in the app explains these limits, and the same explanation is repeated in the app rather than hidden on a website.

Those are real mitigations, and they are not enough on their own. A warning label does not make bad advice safe to follow, and we would rather you use the app sceptically than treat it as though the warning is all we have done.

What we send to the AI provider

When you use a generated feature, the text you submit and the minimum context needed to answer it are sent to a third-party model provider — OpenAI, and Google for video and photo form analysis. That context is one athlete summary the app calls your dossier: your sport, level, age band, goals, personal bests, recent sessions and training load, recovery and readiness, any injuries you recorded, the last week of food you logged and any saved nutrition targets, breaks or events you recorded that affect your training, and anything you saved as a coach memory. You can see exactly which sections it holds, and refresh it, from What the coach knows about you on the coach screen. The Privacy Policy explains this in full.

Do not type anything into these features that you would not want processed that way. If that is a problem for what you need help with, do not use the feature.

What we do not do

  • We do not train models on your data ourselves. What a provider does with a request once it reaches them is governed by that provider's own API terms. We have not set a zero-retention or no-training option on our requests, so the app does not promise that your AI input is never stored or never used for model improvement. If a provider offers you a way to object to that use, you exercise it with that provider directly — we cannot do it for you. AI Data Use & Processing and the Privacy Policy state exactly what is and is not promised.
  • We do not use your health data to target advertising at you.
  • We do not make automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you. There is no automated profiling that decides whether you get a job, a price, a visa, or anything comparable.
