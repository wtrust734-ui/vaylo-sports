The short version. Vaylo Sports is intended for adult athletes aged 18 to 30. It is not age-gated, and an athlete of 13 or over can hold an account, so this page sets out what changes for an under-18 account: the app switches off the things an under-18 athlete can be harmed by — advertising, nutrition and weight advice, and public profiles — and it caps training volume by age. 1. Who this app is for

Vaylo Sports is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone in that group. If you are under 13, please do not use the app, and if you have, tell us and we will delete the account.

The app is intended for 18–30-year-old athletes — from developing athletes through serious and competitive ones. That is the market it is designed and written for. It is not a technical age gate: we do not verify age or identity, and we do not block anyone by age. Our Terms set the minimum age for holding an account at 13.

Because an under-18 account is possible, it is treated differently from an adult one in the ways set out below. We do not ask for parental consent to use the app at 13 or above; where the law requires it for your country, this section does not override that requirement. Nothing on this page claims certification under the Children's Code, COPPA or any other scheme.

These are enforced in code and in the database, not just hidden in the interface. A bug in a screen cannot expose an under-18 athlete to an advert.

One honest limitation: we rely on the date of birth you give us. If an adult enters a child's date of birth, or an under-18 enters an adult's, the wrong protections will apply. We have no way to verify this, and we are not collecting any extra information to try, because that would be a bigger intrusion than the problem warrants. This is a business decision you should be aware of, not a solved problem.

  • Under-18s are also given a deload week more often — every 3–4 weeks rather than every 4–6.
  • Overtraining is flagged before it is a problem. If an under-18 athlete's weekly load goes above a safe threshold, the app raises an overtraining alert against that athlete's own data.
  • Age is derived from your date of birth, so if it is wrong these limits are wrong too.
A cap is not a plan. These limits stop the app from suggesting an unreasonable week. They do not know that you are growing, that your growth spurt is coming, that you have a condition, that you are returning from injury, or that you play three sports. Use them as a ceiling, not a target. If in doubt, ask a coach who knows you — and if it hurts, ask a doctor.

4. Contact between adults and under-18s

Vaylo Sports does not have private messaging between athletes, and there is no facility for an adult to contact a user of the app. We do not run a direct-message system, so there is no message inbox to abuse. If that ever changes, this page changes with it.

The surfaces an under-18 athlete can be reached on are: the public feed, communities, challenges and leaderboards — and only if they choose to share anything, which the private-by-default rule above means an under-18 generally does not. They can also send friend requests.

If an adult contacts an under-18 athlete, we treat it as harassment. The athlete should block them and report it, and we remove the content and act against the account. See the Community Guidelines.

5. Who is responsible for an under-18 account

The account belongs to the athlete who created it, and we have no way to verify who that is. We do hold guardian links, and it is worth being precise about what they are: an athlete creates a short single-use code in the app, whoever redeems it becomes the linked guardian, and the link stays pending until the athlete approves it. An account held for a child under 13 is linked immediately instead, because in that case the adult is the person responsible for it. The athlete chooses what a linked guardian can see, can change it, and can revoke the link at any time. A linked guardian sees a reduced summary — load and recovery bands, and safety alerts — never the training log itself. There is no separate parental-controls dashboard beyond that.

For an under-18 account, we think the honest arrangement is:

  • Tell a parent or guardian that you use the app, and let them see what you are training. Linking them with the code above is how to do that, and it is still the single most useful thing you can do.
  • A parent or guardian can ask us to delete the account at any time, with no reason needed. Email %%CONTACT_EMAIL%% and we will do it.
  • Your coach's plan beats our numbers. If the app disagrees with your coach, follow your coach.

6. Reporting a child safety concern

We take child safety reports seriously and act on them as a priority. There are three routes:

ConcernWhere to go
A child is being harmed or is at riskThe police, or the NSPCC Child Protection Helpline on 0808 8000 111. Do not wait for us.
Content sexualising a child is on the appThe police and the NSPCC helpline above, and email us so we can remove the account immediately
Adults contacting athletes under 18, bullying, dangerous or disordered-eating contentIn-app Report control, and/or email us
An adult has created an account for a child under 13Email us and we will delete it
Our inbox is not an emergency service. We read every report, but we are a small team and we are not child protection specialists. If a child is in danger, contact the police or the NSPCC. Where the law permits or requires it, we will report child sexual exploitation material, credible threats of violence, or serious self-harm content to the relevant authority ourselves, whether or not the reporter asks us to.

7. Privacy for under-18s

  • Private by default. New accounts default to private across every visibility setting, and an account held by an under-18 cannot be made public at all.
  • Nothing about you reaches a brand. Athletes under 18 are excluded from aggregate sponsor reporting entirely.
  • Minimised by design. Under-18s get no nutrition or calorie features, which is also the most sensitive data category in the app.
  • The usual rights apply, and so do your choices. Access, correction, deletion and portability all work the same way. See Your Data Rights and Deleting Your Account.

8. What we have not yet finalised

We would rather list the gaps than imply we have covered them:

  • A response time for child safety reports. %%OPERATOR_NAME%% has not yet committed to a turnaround time, so we are not publishing one we would not keep.
  • A named child protection lead. There is no individual we can yet point you to.
  • Age assurance. We rely on self-declared date of birth and have not implemented age estimation or ID checks.
  • Parent or guardian accounts. There is no separate guardian account type. A guardian uses their own Vaylo Sports account and a link the athlete approves, which is described in section 5.
  • Annual child safety reporting to the ICO. If we meet the threshold, %%OPERATOR_NAME%% will need to produce this. It is a business decision that has not yet been taken.

None of this makes the app safe for children on its own, and none of this page limits our legal responsibility for it. What it does is tell you exactly where the protections stop.

9. Children's Privacy Notice

This is the notice for a parent, guardian or carer who is responsible for a child using Vaylo Sports. It is part of this page rather than a separate document, so that the protections described above and the notice describing them cannot drift apart.

Who this covers. Athletes aged 13 to 17. The app is intended for adult athletes aged 18 to 30; an under-18 account is possible because we do not verify age, and this notice describes what we do with one. Vaylo Sports is not directed at children under 13 and they should not create an account. We do not knowingly collect data from a child under 13; if we learn that we have, we delete it.

What we collect from an under-18 athlete. The training data they log, the health and wearable data they choose to connect, the content they choose to post, and their account details. It is data the app needs to do the job, not a profile built up about them.

What we do not do with it. We do not sell it. We do not use it for advertising or profiling. We exclude athletes under 18 entirely from anything reported to a sponsor or brand — their activity is never counted in an aggregate a company sees. We do not show nutrition or weight content to them at all.

What we have switched off for them: advertising and sponsored content, nutrition and calorie features, every promotional nudge (discount offers, bonus-credit pushes and product recommendations), and public profiles. The first two and the last are enforced in the database, not just in the interface — see section 2. Buying itself is open to them, because a refusal we cannot police would only push a determined under-18 elsewhere; instead a purchase on an under-18 account must involve a parent or guardian and is refunded in full on request, whatever the circumstances — see Refunds and Cancellations and Credits and Subscriptions.

Who can see it. The account data is private to the athlete and to a guardian the athlete has linked, who sees only the reduced summary described in section 5. It is not shown on a public profile. Other athletes can see shared activity in the feed, which is why the default is to share nothing.

Your rights as a parent or guardian. You can ask what we hold, ask us to correct it, ask us to stop a particular use, and ask us to delete the account entirely. Deletion needs no reason and no justification, and you do not need the athlete's agreement to ask for it. If the athlete has linked you to the account, you can also see the reduced summary described in section 5 without asking us. Email %%CONTACT_EMAIL%% or see Deleting Your Account and Your Data Rights.

If you are worried about a child's safety rather than their data, use Report a Safety Concern, which is the more urgent route. If a child is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.

An honest limitation. We rely on the date of birth an athlete gives us. We have no way to verify it, and we are not collecting extra information to try, because that would be a bigger intrusion than the problem warrants. That means the protections on this page depend on that date of birth being accurate, and an under-18 athlete who enters an adult's date of birth will not receive them.
