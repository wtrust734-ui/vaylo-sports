Report a Safety Concern

Version 1.0 · 2 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. If something in Vaylo Sports has made you or someone else unsafe, or you are worried a child is, you can tell us. You do not need an account to email us, you do not need to explain why, and you do not need to have proof. We would rather hear about a concern that turns out to be nothing than miss one that turns out to be serious.

What counts as a safety concern

Email vaylosportssupport@gmail.com with Safety in the subject line if you want it treated as urgent. A concern is anything like:

  • an under-18 athlete is being harmed, harassed, groomed, or is at risk;
  • an adult is contacting an under-18 athlete, or an athlete is being pressured into sending images, contact details or anything else;
  • content on the feed, in a community, in a challenge, or from a coach or creator that is unsafe, sexual, hateful, or promotes self-harm or dangerous behaviour;
  • unsafe coaching, unsafe training advice, or a feature that pushed someone toward overtraining, disordered eating, or ignoring an injury;
  • an AI feature that produced something dangerous;
  • an adult using the app to find or contact a child;
  • anything that made you think someone was in danger.

You do not have to be sure it is a safety issue. If it felt wrong, send it.

If a child is at risk

If a child is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. Do not wait for a reply from us. We are an app, not an emergency service, and we cannot dispatch help.

After that, or if the danger is not immediate, tell us as well. In the UK you can also report a child at risk to the NSPCC or to the ICO; in the US, the NCMEC CyberTipline is the reporting route for child exploitation. We would rather you used every route available than only ours.

We treat a report about an under-18 athlete as more urgent than an ordinary support request, and it goes to a human rather than into a support queue. We do not currently have a staffed moderation team working around the clock, so if a report arrives outside UK working hours it may not be read until the next working day. That is a real limitation, and it is the reason we tell you to use the emergency and official routes above when there is immediate danger.

What helps us act on it

Include whatever you have. All of it is useful, including things that feel too small to mention:

  • the username of the athlete or account involved, and the content — a link, a screenshot, or a description of what it said;
  • roughly when it happened;
  • whether a child is involved, and their approximate age if you know it;
  • whether anyone is in immediate danger;
  • how we can reply to you, and whether that contact is safe.

If you are reporting for someone else, please say so and tell us how they found out. It changes how carefully we have to handle it.

You can report anonymously by leaving your contact details off the email. We will still be able to act on the report, but we will not be able to come back to you with questions, and we will not be able to tell you the outcome.

What we do when we receive one

In rough order, and without promising a particular outcome:

  1. We read it, and we judge whether a child is at risk.
  2. If a child may be at risk, we act on the account first — limiting or suspending access can happen before any investigation is finished, and before we have spoken to anyone.
  3. We look at the content and the account involved.
  4. We decide what action is appropriate: a warning, a limit, a suspension, or removal of the content.
  5. We tell the person reported what we decided and why.
  6. We feed what we learned back into the product where we can.

We do not promise a particular response time. We would rather say nothing here than publish a number we would not keep. We also do not run a 24-hour moderation service, and we do not have a published response-time commitment.

What we cannot do

We want to be straight about the limits of reporting to us:

  • We cannot investigate outside our own app. If the harm happened elsewhere, we can only act on the account here and we will tell you that.
  • We cannot guarantee removal. Sometimes content is not against these terms, or removing it would remove evidence, or the account is genuine and the report is mistaken.
  • We cannot always tell you the outcome. If you report anonymously, or you would rather we did not, we will not come back to you.
  • We cannot restore a limit or a suspension on request alone. If you think we got it wrong, use the complaints procedure.
  • We cannot monitor the app in real time. Nothing here is a substitute for supervision of an under-18 athlete by an adult.

Reporting is never held against you. We do not retaliate against anyone for making a report, and we do not tell the person reported who reported them.

Other ways to report

You are not restricted to us, and for some things you should not use us at all:

  • Content in the app, by an athlete — use the Report control in the app. See Report Content or a User. That is faster than emailing and reaches the same place.
  • Your own data or privacy — see Your Data Rights.
  • A security flaw in the app — see Security & Vulnerability Reporting, which has a safe-harbour promise and is the right route for that.
  • Something that happened off-platform — the police, or the appropriate child-protection body in your country.
