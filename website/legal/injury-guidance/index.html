Injury & Return-to-Training Guidance

Version 1.1 · 8 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. The app helps you record an injury and notice changes over time. It cannot assess an injury, diagnose one, or tell you when it is safe to train again. Only a qualified professional who has examined you can do that, and only they can clear you to return.

Vaylo Sports is intended for adult athletes aged 18–30, and this guidance is written for them. That is not a technical age gate — age is not verified — and where an account shows an athlete is under 18, the age-based limits described in Child & Young Athlete Safety stay switched on.

What the app does with injuries

You can log an injury: what happened, where you felt it, when it started, and how it is going. The app keeps that history so you can see whether something is settling or getting worse, and so you can describe it accurately to a professional instead of trying to remember.

That is the whole of it. The app records; it does not assess. It does not diagnose, does not estimate severity, does not calculate a return date, and does not tell you whether to stop training.

What it does not do

Nothing in the app is a medical assessment, and nothing in it clears you to train. If you have been injured, the decision about what you do next belongs to a doctor, a physiotherapist, or a qualified sports physiotherapist — a person who has actually examined you.
  • No diagnosis. The app cannot identify what is wrong.
  • No severity scoring. Nothing here tells you how serious something is.
  • No return-to-play date. We do not generate one, because a date generated from a log is a date with no clinical basis.
  • No clearance. An improving entry in the app is not permission to resume, and a cleared entry is not permission to progress.
  • No substitute for being looked at. Palpation, range of movement, strength testing and imaging are things a person does to you.

When to stop training

This is the most important section on the page. Stop and get advice if you have:

  • chest pain, or pain that spreads to your arm, neck or jaw;
  • unexplained breathlessness, or breathlessness that does not settle;
  • fainting, or feeling like you are about to pass out;
  • a fever alongside training, or a fever that appears after hard sessions;
  • sharp or sudden pain, or a pop followed by pain;
  • pain that changes how you move — a limp, or guarding;
  • pain that is getting worse over days rather than settling;
  • swelling that is increasing, or appears within hours of a session;
  • any symptom that worries you, which is reason enough on its own.
If any of those is happening now and it is severe, contact your local emergency services. Do not wait for the app, and do not wait for us.

Do not train through pain to keep a streak, a rank or a challenge position. Nothing in this app is worth an injury, and losing a streak is a far smaller cost than the alternative. The streak feature is not designed to make you feel you have something to prove.

Coming back, in outline

Every injury is different and the detail belongs to your clinician, but the general shape of a return is consistent enough to describe:

  1. Rest until the acute stage has passed and someone qualified has assessed you. This is not optional and not negotiable by feel.
  2. Restore movement pain-free, before loading it. Regaining range usually comes before regaining strength.
  3. Rebuild strength, beginning well below where you were.
  4. Reintroduce load gradually, and expect less than you think for longer than you want.
  5. Return to speed and change of direction later than to gym work, and later than most people expect.
  6. Reintroduce full training and competition last.

The specific order and timing for you come from your professional, and where a sport or a governing body publishes return-to-play protocols, those take precedence over general advice.

You can log what you do through this in the app so the progression is visible across weeks. The record is useful; it is not the plan.

Recurring injuries and getting second opinions

If something keeps coming back, that is a reason to see a professional sooner, not later. Repeated injury is often a sign that something is not being addressed — the load, the technique, the recovery, the equipment, or a cause nobody has looked at yet.

You are entitled to a second opinion, and you do not need our permission or a reason to seek one. If you are not being taken seriously, that is worth saying plainly to a clinician rather than continuing to manage it alone.

For an under-18 athlete, involve a parent or guardian and, where there is one, a club or school physio. See Child & Young Athlete Safety.

Your injury records

An injury record is health data. It is private to you, it is not shown on your profile, and it is deleted with your account. It is included in a data export so you can take it to a clinician, and it is never sold or used for advertising.

If you need it shared with a club, a coach or a doctor, that is your decision and your action — we do not share it with anyone. The detail is in the Privacy Policy and in Your Data Rights.

Do not enter real medical records or documents here. The injury log is for your own notes, not a medical record.
