Nutrition Disclaimer

Version 1.1 · 8 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. Vaylo Sports is a training log. It is not a diet, not a nutrition service, and not a place to get help with an eating disorder or a medical condition. Nothing in it is medical nutrition advice. For athletes under 18, the nutrition and calorie features are switched off entirely.

Vaylo Sports is intended for adult athletes aged 18–30, and this notice is written for them. That is not a technical age gate — age is not verified — and where an account shows an athlete is under 18, the nutrition and calorie features stay switched off and the other age-based limits in Child & Young Athlete Safety stay in place.

The current state of nutrition in the app

For athletes under 18, the nutrition and calorie features are switched off entirely, in code, and cannot be turned on. That is deliberate. Restrictive eating advice is a genuine danger for athletes under 18, and we would rather have no feature than a harmful one. It is enforced in the app rather than only hidden in the interface. See Child & Young Athlete Safety.

For athletes aged 18 and over, the app does two separate things, and it is worth being precise about which is which.

  • A daily figure, calculated from what you tell it. Before a plan is written the app asks a set of questions — your weight and height, how many days a week you train and for how long, how active the rest of your day is, the goal you are training for, any dietary pattern, and any food you avoid or react to. From those answers it derives a starting calorie and macronutrient figure (Mifflin-St Jeor for base metabolism, an activity factor built from your own training week, then the goal) and shows you the working, so you can see which answer moved the number and override it.
  • A generated plan of meals. The week-by-week menus are written by a language model from the same answers. They are suggestions of ordinary food, and they can be wrong about portions, or unsuitable for you for a reason the app does not know.

Both are estimates, and both are 18+ only. They are not a dietetic assessment, they are not built from your medical history, and they cannot account for a condition you have not told us about. Weight and height can be left blank — the app then names the assumed values it used rather than guessing silently.

We do not set weight goals and we do not monitor your intake. The goal you pick — a cut, maintenance or a bulk — is your decision, and the app applies a deliberately modest adjustment to the maintenance figure it calculated; it never tells you what you should weigh. It reads only the food you choose to log, and only when you ask for a review of it. Nothing is logged for you and nothing is tracked in the background.

We are not a nutrition service and we do not provide medical nutrition therapy. For anything clinical, a registered dietitian is the right person, and this page is not a substitute for that conversation.

What this is not

  • Not medical nutrition therapy. That is the work of a registered dietitian or a doctor, and it is regulated differently from anything an app can offer.
  • Not a weight loss or weight gain plan. We do not set a target weight, and nothing here tells you what you should weigh. Where a daily intake is suggested it is movement on your own maintenance figure, under a goal you chose, and it is deliberately modest rather than dramatic.
  • Not safe for disordered eating. Calorie information, weight targets and food talk can be actively harmful to someone with an eating disorder or a history of one. If that describes you, please do not use the nutrition material.
  • Not suitable for pregnancy, diabetes, coeliac disease, allergies, IBS, or any other condition where diet and health interact. Those need a clinician.
  • Not a sports science protocol. Where a sport's governing body publishes fuelling guidance, that is the authority, not an app.

A specific warning about under-eating

If you are eating less than you need in order to train, that is a health risk, not a discipline. Chronic under-fuelling in athletes is associated with low energy availability, which affects bone density, the menstrual cycle, immune function, mood, injury risk and performance. It is not sustainable and it is not a shortcut.

The pressure to under-eat in sport is real, and it often comes from the sport culture rather than from anyone in particular. Weight is a poor proxy for readiness, and a scale tells you almost nothing about whether you are healthy.

Signs it may be worth talking to someone — persistent fatigue, injuries that keep recurring, a drop in performance that training has not explained, feeling cold or dizzy, loss of bone density, menstrual changes, or a preoccupation with food. A registered dietitian or a doctor is the right person. So is a parent, a guardian, a school physio, or a trusted coach.

If food and weight are affecting your mental health, please talk to someone. There is more in Mental Wellbeing.

Where to get real help

For anything beyond general information:

  • a registered dietitian for sports or clinical nutrition;
  • a doctor for anything involving a medical condition;
  • an eating disorder specialist if food and weight are becoming compulsive, or if you are restricting;
  • your sport's governing body for sanctioned fuelling guidance.

We are an app. We cannot assess your nutrition, and we will not pretend otherwise.
