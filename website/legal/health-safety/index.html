Training & Health Safety Notice

Version 1.1 · 8 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

Vaylo Sports is not a medical device and is not professional advice. It cannot see you, cannot examine you, and does not know your medical history. Do not use it to diagnose, treat, or manage an injury or a medical condition.

Nothing in the app decides whether you are fit to train or to compete. It cannot assess medical fitness, and no output here is a medical clearance to train, to return from an injury, or to compete. Only a qualified professional who has examined you can give you that.

In an emergency, contact emergency services first. This app is not a route to urgent help.

Vaylo Sports is intended for adult athletes aged 18–30, and this notice is written for them. That is not a technical age gate — age is not verified — and where an account shows an athlete is under 18, the age-based limits described below stay switched on.

What the numbers in this app actually mean

Vaylo calculates training load, recovery, readiness and your VPR rating from the information you enter. They are descriptions of your data, not measurements of your body. This matters more than it sounds:

  • A readiness score of 40 does not mean you are injured. 90 does not mean you are fine. These are your own inputs, run through a formula.
  • If you log everything as "felt great", the app has nothing to work with and will cheerfully tell you everything is fine.
  • The app cannot stop you doing too much. It does not know what you did off-platform, and it is not watching you.

Training safely

  • Build up slowly. Most training injuries come from increasing volume or intensity faster than tissue adapts. The app cannot slow you down.
  • Log rest honestly. Rest days and easy days are data. Logging only the hard sessions makes every number wrong.
  • Under-18s get age-adjusted loads automatically. Do not work around that because a plan looks light — the adjustment exists because the evidence says athletes under 18 are not small adults.
  • Pain is information. Sharp, escalating, or persistent pain is a reason to stop and see someone, not a training adaptation to push through.
  • Recovery is not optional. If the app tells you your recovery is poor and you train through it anyway, the app is not the reason you were injured — but it will not have warned you off.
  • Hydration and nutrition are yours to judge. The app records what you tell it. It cannot tell you whether you are eating enough, and it cannot see a disordered pattern.

Challenges and leaderboards are motivation, not prescription

Vaylo has challenges, streaks and leaderboards. The app says so on the screen itself. They are community goals for motivation and bragging rights. Do not use one as a training target while injured or recovering, and do not let a leaderboard position push you past what your body can do. If a challenge or a brand promotion conflicts with what a coach or clinician has told you, they lose.

Injury Management: what it is and is not

The Injury feature is a generic, evidence-led return-to-play scaffold. It is not a diagnosis, not a treatment, and not tailored to your injury by anyone who has examined you. It carries a permanent on-screen notice directing anyone with sharp, persistent or worsening pain to a physiotherapist or doctor. It is a credit-gated feature and is one of the parts of the app most likely to be wrong about your specific situation.

Nutrition: 18+ only

Nutrition planning in Vaylo is restricted to athletes aged 18 and over, enforced in the app's own code with a refusal message. This is deliberate. Restrictive or disordered eating is a serious risk in athletes under 18, and a generated eating plan is the wrong tool for anyone in that group. If you are under 18, do not work around this.

Health data you provide

Recovery check-ins, soreness, sleep, mood, injuries, height and weight are health data. We use them to show you your own trends, and — only where a generated feature is switched on — to give the AI context about you. We do not use health data for advertising, we do not sell it, and it is not included in any sponsorship reporting. The Privacy Policy covers this in detail, including how to withdraw consent and how to delete it.

Connecting a wearable or Health Connect

These integrations are not currently active. The code to read from Health Connect and from providers such as Strava, Garmin, Fitbit, Oura, WHOOP, Polar, Suunto and COROS exists, but the developer agreements those providers require have not been signed, so the connections are switched off in the app rather than half-working. If and when they are enabled, the Privacy Policy describes exactly what each one would read, and you authorise each through that provider's own consent screen and can revoke it there.

Where the app does measure distance during a tracked workout, it calculates on your device and stores only the resulting distance — not your route or coordinates. That feature is also currently switched off in the Android build.

Training safety guidelines

Practical rules for training through this app without doing yourself harm. They are general, they are not tailored to you, and they do not replace a coach or a clinician who knows you.

  • Progress gradually. Most injuries in amateur sport come from a sudden jump in volume or intensity, not from the training itself. If a number goes up a lot, that is a reason for caution, not ambition.
  • Build one thing at a time. If you add volume, hold intensity. If you add intensity, hold volume. You cannot tell which change caused a problem if you changed both.
  • Rest is training. A plan with no rest days in it is not a plan.
  • Sleep before you add load. Persistent poor sleep and rising training load is a combination worth taking seriously.
  • Eat enough to train. Chronic under-fuelling affects bone density, immunity, mood, injury risk and performance. It is not a discipline.
  • Fuel the hard sessions. The heaviest days need the most fuel, not the least.
  • Do not increase load in the week you return from injury or illness.
  • Use a plan you would actually follow. The best plan is the one you will do, not the one that looks most impressive.
  • Let a week of data be a week of data. One session, or one bad night, is not a trend. The app will happily tell you something is wrong because you had a single unusual day.

For injury specifically, see Injury & Return-to-Training. For nutrition, the nutrition disclaimer. For when to stop and get help, see Injury & Return-to-Training.

Children and under-18s

Read the Child & Young Athlete Safety policy. It covers what changes for under-18s, what we do to keep profiles private by default, and what to do if training pressure is coming from an adult.

If you are under 18 and someone is telling you to restrict food, train through pain, or hide how much you are doing — that is not normal coaching. Talk to a parent, a teacher, a school nurse, or a doctor. You can also email us at vaylosportssupport@gmail.com.
