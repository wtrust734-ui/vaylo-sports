Report Content or a User

Version 1.0 · 2 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. Every profile, feed post and comment has a Report control. One tap opens a short form, you pick a reason, and it is filed. Reports are private to you, they are rate-limited so nobody can flood the queue, and the status of a report is set by us rather than by the person reporting.
On this page
  1. How to report
  2. What happens to your report
  3. What we do about it
  4. Blocking, and what it does

How to report

Open the profile, post or comment, use the Report control, and choose a reason. The reasons are a fixed list, not a free-text box, because a moderator has to be able to act on a report and an unlabelled one is much harder to action:

  • harassment, bullying or threats;
  • hate — racist, sexist, homophobic or otherwise abusive;
  • sexual content, or sexualising a minor;
  • self-harm content;
  • dangerous content — encouraging unsafe training or unsafe eating;
  • spam, advertising or scams;
  • pretending to be Vaylo Sports or another athlete;
  • someone's personal information posted without their consent;
  • anything else.

You can add a short description. It is optional, and it is the part a human reads first, so it genuinely helps. Keep it under a thousand characters.

You do not need to prove anything. An unproven report is still a report.

What happens to your report

Filing a report creates a record that only you can see. It is stored with the content you reported and the reason you gave, and it is never shown to the person you reported. The status of a report is changed by us, not by you and not by the person reported.

Tapping report twice on the same thing does not create a second report; you will just see the one you already made. There is a limit of ten reports an hour per account, which is far above any genuine use and exists so that nobody can bury a real report under noise.

You cannot see the outcome, and you cannot delete the report. That is deliberate. A report is an accusation; letting the person who made it withdraw it to hide it, or telling them what happened, would protect the reporter less, not more.

What we do about it

Every report is read. Depending on what we find, we may take no action, send a warning, limit what an account can do, remove the content, or suspend the account. For an under-18 athlete, a report about adult contact is treated as urgent and the account is limited first while we look.

We do not promise that content will be removed. Some reports are mistaken, some content is not against the Community Guidelines, and occasionally we keep a post up because removing it would destroy evidence of a safety problem. If we do nothing, that is a decision and not an oversight.

The person whose content was removed is told what we decided and why, and can use the complaints procedure if they disagree.

Blocking, and what it does

Alongside reporting there is a Block control. Blocking removes that athlete's shared activity from your feed, and it happens on our servers rather than on your device — the rows are not sent to you in the first place, which is the only version of blocking that is actually private.

Blocking works in both directions for the feed. If you block someone, their activity stops appearing for you; if someone blocks you, yours stops appearing for them. We do that deliberately, because otherwise blocking could be defeated just by blocking back.

Blocking is not anonymous as far as we are concerned — we know an account blocked another — and we do not tell the blocked person that it happened. Blocking is not a report. If you block someone because of how they behaved, please also report it.

Limits of this system

Being straight about what reporting and blocking do not achieve:

  • They do not act outside the app. We can only act on accounts here.
  • They are not real-time moderation. Reports are read by people, not by a filter watching every post as it is made.
  • They do not remove content from other people's devices that they have already seen.
  • They are not a substitute for supervision. An under-18 athlete's account should be supervised by an adult; see Child & Young Athlete Safety.

If a report is about a child being at risk, use Report a Safety Concern as well — that route is treated more urgently than an in-app report.
