Mental Wellbeing & Healthy Training Guidance

Version 1.1 · 8 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. The wellbeing features are a place to note how training is affecting you. They are not a mental health service, they do not screen for anything, and nobody at Vaylo Sports is monitoring your check-ins. If you are struggling, please talk to a person who can help.

Vaylo Sports is intended for adult athletes aged 18–30, and this guidance is written for them. That is not a technical age gate — age is not verified — and where an account shows an athlete is under 18, the age-based limits described in Child & Young Athlete Safety stay switched on.

What the wellbeing features are

The app lets you record how you are — mood, energy, sleep, stress — alongside your training, and look at it over time. The point is to notice a pattern: that a particular block of training is costing you more than it is giving, that sleep and mood move together, that a heavy week was heavier than the numbers suggested.

It is a journal with some context, not a clinical tool. It is private to you.

Mental Gym is the other half, and it is a set of tools rather than a record: a daily check-in, a paced breathing tool (box, calm, and a short reset, all at a breathing rate slow enough to settle you rather than raise your heart rate), pressure-scenario rehearsal, a cognitive reset, cue words, and an Event Mode for competition day. Event Mode is built for a day with more than one event in it: a guided breath you can start at any point, a pre-event checklist that resets each morning, a list of the day's events with a live countdown to the next one, a between-events reset for the gap, and somewhere to write what worked and what to fix afterwards.

What they are not — including the gap we cannot paper over

The wellbeing features are not mental health support, not screening, and not monitoring. Nobody at Vaylo Sports reads your check-ins. There is no one watching for a disclosure of distress. The app will not contact you, alert anyone, or escalate anything.

We want to be direct about this rather than let the design imply a safeguard that does not exist. If you write something in the app saying you are not safe, or you are thinking about harming yourself, nothing will happen. No one is on the other end of it.

That is a genuine limitation, and it is one we have chosen to state rather than dress up. The alternative — wording that suggests someone is paying attention when they are not — would be worse, because it would be exactly the kind of false assurance that makes someone wait.

So the wellbeing features are for self-reflection, not support. If you need support, the routes below are the real ones.

If you need help now

If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please contact your local emergency services or a crisis line in your country now. Do not wait for a reply from us, and do not use the app as the first step.

We are an app operated by a small team. We are not a crisis service and we cannot provide one. We are not going to publish a phone number here that we cannot answer ourselves — that is not a safety measure, it is a way of feeling better about the gap.

What will actually reach someone:

  • Emergency services in your country, if you are in immediate danger.
  • A crisis or mental health helpline in your country. In the UK, NHS 111 and Samaritans are reachable; in the US, 988 is the national suicide and crisis lifeline. Elsewhere, your local health service will know the number.
  • A doctor, who can also refer you to mental health support.
  • Someone you trust — a friend, a family member, a partner, a teacher, a coach. Saying "I am not okay" out loud to another person is a real intervention.

For an under-18 athlete, tell an adult you trust. That could be a parent or guardian, a teacher, a school counsellor, or a club welfare lead. See Child & Young Athlete Safety.

Training and mental health

Training and mental health affect each other in both directions, and the relationship is well established. Some things worth knowing:

  • Under-training and over-training both affect mood. So does training through illness.
  • Injury is a mental health event, not just a physical one. Loss of identity, isolation from a team, and fear of re-injury are common and are not signs of weakness.
  • Comparison is a feature of sport and a tax on wellbeing. Leaderboards are there to be motivating. If yours is not, do not enter them; nothing in the app requires it.
  • Compulsion and streak mechanics can go wrong. If maintaining a streak has become something that drives you past what is sensible, delete the app. There is nothing in it worth that.
  • Performance is not a measure of worth. A bad week is a week, not a verdict.

If training is making you worse rather than better, the right move is to stop or reduce, and to talk to someone. Nothing in this app is designed to keep you training.

Your wellbeing records

Mood, energy and sleep entries are health data. They are private to you, are not shown on your profile or in the feed, are not visible to coaches or creators, and are deleted with your account. They are included in a data export and are never used for advertising.

One part of that is deliberately not a record in your account at all: everything you do inside Event Mode — the checklist ticks, the event list for the day, the cue word you pinned and the notes you write after a performance — is held in local storage on the device you used. None of it is uploaded, none of it is sent to a model, and clearing the app's data removes it.

Because they are so private, that is also a limit: nobody else can see them, including anyone who would want to know you are struggling. If you want support, you have to tell a person. See Privacy Policy and Your Data Rights.
