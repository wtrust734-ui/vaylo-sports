Wearable & Health Data Privacy Notice

Version 1.0 · 2 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. If you connect a fitness wearable, we receive the activity and health data you choose to share so the app can use it. We do not sell it, we do not share it with advertisers or sponsors, and you can disconnect at any time. Your wearable data is among the most sensitive data the app holds, and we treat it that way.

  1. Connecting a wearable
  2. What we receive, and what we do with it
  3. Accuracy, and why it matters

Connecting a wearable

The app can read activity and health data from a connected wearable or health platform. Connecting happens through the provider's own authorisation screen — the app does not see or store your provider password, and the authorisation is revoked from the provider's side as well as ours.

Connecting is entirely optional. Every feature that uses wearable data can be used without it. Nothing about your account is worse if you never connect one.

What is available depends on the provider and on the permissions you grant. The app declares only the health permissions it actually uses, and if you decline one, the related feature is switched off rather than silently broken.

What we receive, and what we do with it

Depending on what you authorise, that can include steps, distance, heart rate, sleep, workouts and calories. We use it to:

  • fill in your training log, so you are not retyping what your watch already knows;
  • calculate load, recovery and readiness, which is arithmetic on data you already have;
  • show you trends over time.

What we do not do with it:

  • We do not sell it. There is no sale of personal data, to anyone, for any price.
  • We do not share it with advertisers or brands, and it is excluded from anything reported about an under-18 athlete.
  • We do not use it for profiling or targeted advertising.
  • We do not use it to infer anything about you that you did not ask to be measured.

The full list is in the Privacy Policy.

Accuracy, and why it matters

Wearable data is an estimate, and the estimate is not always good. Heart rate at interval pace, sleep staging, calorie burn and GPS distance all carry meaningful error, and the error is larger on some bodies and devices than on others.

Do not treat a wearable reading as a measurement of your health. It is a consumer device doing its best. If a number from it concerns you, check it against how you feel and, if it still concerns you, against something more reliable — a clinical device, or a clinician.

Where a readiness or load score in the app is derived partly from wearable data, it inherits that error. It is arithmetic on an estimate, not a clinical measure. See Training & Health Safety.

Do not upload a wearable account you share with a family member and then treat the combined data as your own training.

Your control over it

Disconnecting is immediate and complete from our side. You can remove the connection in settings at any time. After that we stop receiving new data. We also delete the wearable data we have already imported, so disconnecting is not merely a stop — it is a removal.

You can also:

  • export everything we hold, wearable data included, as a JSON file — see Your Data Rights;
  • correct it, or delete your account entirely — see Deleting Your Account;
  • ask us to delete just the wearable data without deleting the account, by emailing vaylosportssupport@gmail.com.

You can also revoke access from the provider's own settings, which stops the data at source.

Wearables and under-18s

Athletes under 18 can connect a wearable, because logging their own training is the point of the app for them. What does not change is the rest of the protection: nothing about an under-18 athlete's wearable data reaches a brand, and none of the sensitive features are switched on for them. See Child & Young Athlete Safety.
