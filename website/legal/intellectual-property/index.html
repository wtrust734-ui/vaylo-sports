Intellectual Property

Version 1.0 · 1 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. You own what you post. We own the app, the name and the writing inside it. You get a personal licence to use the app; you do not get to copy it, resell it or pass it off as yours. If you think we have copied your work, this page tells you exactly where to send it.

1. What Vaylo owns

Vaylo Sports owns, or has the right to use:

  • the Vaylo Sports name, logo and branding;
  • the app's design, layout, code, graphics and interface;
  • the original text inside it, including the Learning Hub material, safety notices and training library content;
  • the software, databases and underlying technology;
  • the VPR, training load, recovery and readiness calculation methods, which are developed and maintained by us.

Using the app gives you a personal, non-transferable, non-exclusive licence to use it on your own devices. That is a permission, not a sale, and it ends if you stop using the app or close your account.

The gap we should name. Vaylo Sports's registered company name, company number and registered address have not yet been inserted into this document, because we will not publish legal particulars we cannot verify. Copyright ownership sits with the entity that holds these rights, and that must be confirmed before this section can be treated as a formal assignment of rights. If it turns out a different entity owns the IP, this page will be corrected.

2. What you own

You keep ownership of everything you post — your shared activities, achievements, goals, notes, profile text, images and videos. We do not claim ownership of your training data, your logs, or your personal bests.

You confirm you have the right to post what you post. If you upload a photo of someone else, that is your responsibility, not ours — and it may also break the Community Guidelines if they did not agree to it.

3. The licence you give us

By posting content, you grant us a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to host, store, display and distribute it. In plain terms: enough permission to run the app and show your content to the people you chose to show it to.

  • It is limited. We are not licensing it to sell, to train a model, to advertise, or to hand to sponsors.
  • It lasts only while the content is in the app. Delete your post and the licence for it ends.
  • Copies already made by others, and copies retained in backups for a short period, are the only exceptions.
  • It ends entirely when you delete your account, along with the content itself.
  • It does not survive us selling your data, because we do not do that.

4. Who owns AI output

AI features in Vaylo Sports are currently switched off. When they are on, the position will be:

  • As far as the law allows, output generated for you is yours to use, including commercially.
  • We make no claim of exclusivity over it. We also give no guarantee that it is unique — a language model can produce output that resembles text generated for someone else, or that resembles something already published.
  • We cannot promise it is free of third-party rights, because we do not control the model's training data and cannot inspect every possibility.
  • Check anything important before you publish it, and do not rely on generated output for medical, legal or financial decisions. See the AI & Coaching Disclaimer.
  • We do not train models on your data and grant no provider permission to use your content for advertising.
An honest limitation. Some jurisdictions do not recognise copyright in purely machine-generated output at all, and in others it is a genuinely unsettled question. We are describing the intention, not promising a legal result. If you need certainty for commercial work, treat AI output as a draft and have a human check it.

5. Content we license in

The app uses open-source software and runs on services owned by others, including Supabase for its database and authentication, and model providers for AI generation. Those components remain the property of their respective owners under their own licences. The app's own original material does not incorporate them in any way that changes their terms.

Where we reproduce third-party material — for example an exercise name, a movement description, or a physiological concept used as general knowledge — we do so for factual and educational reasons, with attribution where it is appropriate to give it.

6. The name and logo

  • Please do not use "Vaylo Sports", the logo, or anything confusingly similar for your own app, product, team or company name. It would be misleading, and we will ask you to stop.
  • You may name us factually when saying truthfully that you use the app, or that you are a Vaylo Sports coach. You may not do so in a way that suggests we endorse you or are connected with you unless we have said so in writing.
  • Screenshots and videos of the app are fine for honest reviews, tutorials and commentary. Please do not present them as your own product.

7. Reverse engineering

  • You may not decompile, disassemble, reverse engineer or attempt to derive the source code of the app, except where that restriction is not permitted by law.
  • You may inspect what the app sends over the network — the traffic is yours — and you may report what you find to us or to a security researcher.
  • Automated scraping of the app or its data at scale is not permitted.
  • Exploiting a vulnerability you have found is not permitted, whether or not we have a bug bounty. Tell us and we will look at it.

8. If you think we have infringed your copyright

If you own the copyright in something and believe it appears in Vaylo Sports without permission, email vaylosportssupport@gmail.com with:

  • identification of the work you say has been copied;
  • where it appears in the app — the screen, or a link;
  • your name, contact details and address;
  • a statement that you have a good-faith belief the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law;
  • a statement, under penalty of perjury where the law provides for it, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are the owner or authorised to act for the owner;
  • your physical or electronic signature.
If it is us who has posted it — that is, another athlete has uploaded your photo or your video to their own profile — use the in-app Report control and choose My information posted without my consent. That is faster than an email, it reaches the right place, and it is the route we review most often. You can do both if you want.

If a valid notice is received we will review it, remove the material where we have no defence, and tell you what we did. Where someone has been wrongly accused we will say so rather than simply act. We do not tolerate frivolous or bad-faith complaints, and we may ask you to supply more detail before acting.
