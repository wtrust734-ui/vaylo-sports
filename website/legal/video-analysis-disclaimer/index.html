Video Analysis Disclaimer

Version 1.0 · 2 October 2026

Operated by Vaylo Sports · Contact: vaylosportssupport@gmail.com

The short version. The video form analysis watches a clip and estimates what your movement looks like. It is an estimate from one camera angle, not a measurement, not an assessment of you as an athlete, and not a medical opinion. It is useful for noticing change. It is not safe to clear yourself to train on.
On this page
  1. What the video analysis does
  2. What it is not
  3. Why it is wrong sometimes
  4. What to do with the result

What the video analysis does

You upload or record a clip of a movement — a lift, a jump, a stride, a throw — and the app produces feedback about the shape of that movement.

The output is a visual estimate derived from the pixels in one video. It describes what appears to be happening in the clip. It does not measure force, load, joint position in three dimensions, or anything the camera cannot see. There is no force plate, no motion-capture volume and no clinician in the loop.

A result therefore reflects what was visible in that clip, from that angle, on that day, at that speed, wearing whatever was being worn.

What it is not

Video analysis is not medical advice, physiotherapy, diagnosis, treatment, or a movement screen by a qualified professional. It cannot examine you, palpate anything, or know your history.
  • Not an injury risk score. Nothing here calculates your chance of getting injured. We do not have the data that would make such a number mean anything.
  • Not return-to-play clearance. A good result is not permission to train. A poor result is not a diagnosis of a problem.
  • Not a substitute for a coach watching you in person, from more than one angle, knowing your sport.
  • Not a performance rating. It is not comparable between people, and it is not a measure of athletic ability.
  • Not a measurement. Where it gives numbers — angles, timings — they are estimates from a 2D image and carry real error.

Why it is wrong sometimes

  • One angle hides a lot. A movement that looks symmetrical from the front is not symmetrical, and the reverse is also true.
  • Camera position and distance change the picture. A phone on a tripod is not a controlled lab.
  • Clothing, lighting and framing all affect the result.
  • Speed matters. A technique that is fine at training pace can look very different at competition speed.
  • It has no idea about pain. It will happily describe a movement that hurts you as technically clean.
  • It cannot see load history, fatigue, sleep, stress or growth, all of which change how you move.
  • It is generated from patterns, so it can reflect the assumptions of the material it was built on — including assumptions that do not fit your body, your age or your sport.

Compare clips of yourself over time rather than comparing yourself to anyone. That is the use it is actually good at.

What to do with the result

  1. Use it to notice change, not to diagnose it. A difference between two of your own clips is a prompt to think.
  2. Take anything that concerns you to a professional — a physiotherapist, a doctor, or a qualified coach in your sport.
  3. Never use it to decide whether to train through pain.
  4. Do not increase load because a result improved. Progress is earned over weeks, not clip to clip.

The Injury & Return-to-Training guidance covers stopping and returning: Injury & Return-to-Training.

Your clips and who can see them

A clip of how you move is health-adjacent data, and we treat it that way. Clips are private to you: they are not shown on your profile, not posted to the feed, and not included in community or leaderboard views. You can delete a clip, and deleting your account deletes them.

Where a feature sends anything to an AI provider to generate feedback, only the clip and the movement being analysed are involved — see AI Data Use & Processing. The full picture is in the Privacy Policy and in Your Data Rights.

Do not upload a clip of anyone else without their permission.
